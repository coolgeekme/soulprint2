# The SoulPrint 10-Axis Persona Model: Theoretical Foundations

This document traces the psychological research, personality science, and communication theory that informed each of the 10 persona axes in the SoulPrint system.

---

## Overview

The SoulPrint Persona Model operates on a simple premise: **the way an AI communicates should be as unique as the person it's talking to.** Rather than forcing every user into the same interaction pattern, the system builds a 10-dimensional personality profile that adapts the AI's tone, depth, challenge level, and emotional awareness to each individual.

The 10 axes were not chosen arbitrarily. Each one maps to established dimensions in personality psychology, communication science, or interpersonal theory — fields with decades of empirical validation. The model synthesizes insights from multiple frameworks rather than adopting any single one wholesale.

---

## The 6 Assessment Pillars

Before the 10 axes, the system measures users across **6 psychological pillars** — broad domains of human behavior that feed into the axis calculations via weighted mappings.

| Pillar | Measures | Research Lineage |
|--------|----------|------------------|
| **Communication** | How someone sends, receives, and processes information | Sociolinguistics; Hall's Context Theory (1976); Tannen's Conversational Style (1984) |
| **Emotional Intelligence** | Self-awareness, emotional regulation, empathy | Salovey & Mayer's EI Model (1990); Goleman's EI Framework (1995) |
| **Decision Making** | Cognitive approach to choices under uncertainty | Kahneman's Dual Process Theory (2011); Behavioral Economics |
| **Social Dynamics** | Interpersonal behavior, trust, conflict navigation | Attachment Theory (Bowlby, 1969); Social Penetration Theory (Altman & Taylor, 1973) |
| **Cognitive Style** | Information processing, learning preferences, mental organization | Kolb's Experiential Learning (1984); Field Dependence/Independence (Witkin, 1962) |
| **Assertiveness** | Boundary-setting, self-advocacy, conflict approach | Assertiveness Training literature (Alberti & Emmons, 1970); Interpersonal Circumplex |

## The 10 Axes: Sources and Rationale

### 1. Directness (0 = Diplomatic → 100 = Blunt)

**What it controls:** Whether the AI eases into topics or cuts straight to the point.

**Research foundations:**

- **High-Context vs. Low-Context Communication (Edward T. Hall, 1976).** Hall's foundational work *Beyond Culture* identified that communication styles exist on a spectrum from high-context (meaning is implicit, indirect, requires reading between the lines) to low-context (meaning is explicit, direct, said plainly). This axis directly operationalizes that spectrum for AI behavior.

- **The Interpersonal Circumplex (Timothy Leary, 1957; Jerry Wiggins, 1979).** The circumplex's vertical axis measures dominance/assertiveness — of which directness is a primary behavioral expression. Individuals high on the dominance axis communicate more directly; those lower use more hedging and indirection.

- **Conversational Style (Deborah Tannen, 1984).** Tannen's research on gendered and cultural communication patterns showed that directness preferences are deeply tied to identity and social context — not simply "good" or "bad" communication. The axis respects this by treating both poles as valid.

- **DISC Behavioral Model.** The D (Dominance) dimension correlates with direct, results-oriented communication.

> Hall, E. T. (1976). *Beyond Culture*. Anchor Books.  
> Leary, T. (1957). *Interpersonal Diagnosis of Personality*. Ronald Press.  
> Wiggins, J. S. (1979). A psychological taxonomy of trait-descriptive terms. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 37(3), 395–412.  
> Tannen, D. (1984). *Conversational Style: Analyzing Talk Among Friends*. Ablex.

### 2. Warmth (0 = Clinical → 100 = Nurturing)

**What it controls:** The emotional temperature of the AI — cold competence vs. genuine personal care.

**Research foundations:**

- **The Interpersonal Circumplex — Communion Axis (Wiggins, 1979).** The horizontal axis of the circumplex (communion/affiliation) directly measures warmth vs. hostility. This is one of the two most fundamental dimensions of how humans perceive and relate to each other.

- **Stereotype Content Model (Susan Fiske et al., 2002).** Fiske's research demonstrated that humans evaluate others on exactly two primary dimensions: **warmth** and **competence**. Warmth judgments happen first and carry more weight in interpersonal dynamics. This axis operationalizes that primal evaluation dimension.

- **Big Five — Agreeableness (Costa & McCrae, 1992).** Agreeableness encompasses trust, altruism, compliance, and tender-mindedness — all facets of interpersonal warmth. High agreeableness individuals prefer warm, cooperative interactions; low agreeableness individuals prefer efficient, task-focused ones.

- **Carl Rogers' Person-Centered Therapy (1951).** Rogers identified "unconditional positive regard" as essential for therapeutic relationships. At the high end, this axis emulates that principle for the AI.

> Fiske, S. T., Cuddy, A. J., Glick, P., & Xu, J. (2002). A model of (often mixed) stereotype content. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 82(6), 878–902.  
> Costa, P. T., & McCrae, R. R. (1992). *Revised NEO Personality Inventory (NEO-PI-R) and NEO Five-Factor Inventory (NEO-FFI) Professional Manual*. Psychological Assessment Resources.  
> Rogers, C. R. (1951). *Client-Centered Therapy*. Houghton Mifflin.

---

### 3. Humor (0 = Serious → 100 = Playful)

**What it controls:** How much levity, wit, and playfulness the AI brings to conversations.

**Research foundations:**

- **Humor Styles Questionnaire (Rod Martin et al., 2003).** Martin identified four distinct humor styles: affiliative, self-enhancing, aggressive, and self-defeating. This axis primarily targets *affiliative humor* — using humor to enhance relationships and put others at ease.

- **Big Five — Extraversion (Positive Emotions Facet).** Playfulness and humor correlate strongly with the positive emotions and gregariousness facets of extraversion. Highly extraverted individuals naturally inject humor into interactions.

- **Psychological Safety (Amy Edmondson, 1999).** Research on team dynamics shows that appropriate humor contributes to psychological safety — the shared belief that a team is safe for interpersonal risk-taking. The AI calibrates its humor to match the user's comfort level.

> Martin, R. A., Puhlik-Doris, P., Larsen, G., Gray, J., & Weir, K. (2003). Individual differences in uses of humor and their relation to psychological well-being. *Journal of Research in Personality*, 37(1), 48–75.  
> Edmondson, A. (1999). Psychological safety and learning behavior in work teams. *Administrative Science Quarterly*, 44(2), 350–383.

---

### 4. Challenge (0 = Always Agrees → 100 = Pushes Back)

**What it controls:** Whether the AI validates or intellectually challenges the user's thinking.

**Research foundations:**

- **Socratic Method (Socrates, ~400 BCE).** The oldest formalization of productive intellectual challenge — asking probing questions to expose contradictions and deepen understanding. At high values, the AI adopts a Socratic role.

- **Productive Conflict (Patrick Lencioni, 2002).** In *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team*, Lencioni identified "fear of conflict" as a primary team dysfunction. Healthy teams engage in unfiltered, passionate debate around ideas. This axis calibrates how much the AI mirrors that dynamic.

- **Intellectual Humility Research.** Emerging research on intellectual humility shows that people who are open to being challenged learn faster and make better decisions — but only when challenge is delivered appropriately. The axis interacts with warmth and formality to ensure challenge is constructive.

- **Growth Mindset (Carol Dweck, 2006).** Dweck's work shows that framing challenges as growth opportunities (rather than judgments) leads to better outcomes. The prompt generator's language at high challenge levels reflects this.

> Lencioni, P. (2002). *The Five Dysfunctions of a Team*. Jossey-Bass.  
> Dweck, C. S. (2006). *Mindset: The New Psychology of Success*. Random House.

---

### 5. Detail (0 = Brief → 100 = Thorough)

**What it controls:** Response depth — quick answers vs. comprehensive explanations.

**Research foundations:**

- **Cognitive Load Theory (John Sweller, 1988).** Sweller's research demonstrated that working memory has finite capacity. Too much information overwhelms; too little leaves gaps. This axis calibrates information density to the user's cognitive preference.

- **Big Five — Conscientiousness (Thoroughness Facet).** Highly conscientious individuals prefer comprehensive, well-organized information. Lower conscientiousness individuals prefer bottom-line summaries.

- **Information Foraging Theory (Pirolli & Card, 1999).** Users follow a cost-benefit calculus when consuming information — they want maximum insight for minimum cognitive effort. The detail axis optimizes this ratio per user.

> Sweller, J. (1988). Cognitive load during problem solving: Effects on learning. *Cognitive Science*, 12(2), 257–285.  
> Pirolli, P., & Card, S. (1999). Information foraging. *Psychological Review*, 106(4), 643–675.

---

### 6. Formality (0 = Casual → 100 = Formal)

**What it controls:** The linguistic register — friend texting vs. business email.

**Research foundations:**

- **Register Theory (Martin Joos, 1961).** In *The Five Clocks*, Joos identified five levels of formality in English: frozen, formal, consultative, casual, and intimate. This axis operationalizes that spectrum for AI output.

- **Politeness Theory (Penelope Brown & Stephen Levinson, 1987).** Brown and Levinson showed that politeness strategies (positive face vs. negative face) directly correlate with formality levels. Higher formality respects social distance; lower formality signals solidarity and closeness.

- **Code-Switching Research.** Sociolinguistic research on code-switching demonstrates that skilled communicators adapt their formality to context. The AI mirrors this by adjusting register to match the user's preference.

> Joos, M. (1961). *The Five Clocks*. Harcourt, Brace & World.  
> Brown, P., & Levinson, S. C. (1987). *Politeness: Some Universals in Language Usage*. Cambridge University Press.

---

### 7. Emotional Depth (0 = Surface-Level → 100 = Deep Reads)

**What it controls:** How much the AI reads between the lines and names emotional undercurrents.

**Research foundations:**

- **Emotional Intelligence — Ability Model (Salovey & Mayer, 1990).** The four-branch model identifies perceiving, using, understanding, and managing emotions as core abilities. This axis controls how much the AI exercises branches 1 and 3 — perceiving emotions and understanding their transitions.

- **Emotional Intelligence — Performance Model (Daniel Goleman, 1995).** Goleman popularized EI as a set of practical competencies: self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, and social skills. The emotional depth axis most directly maps to the *empathy* competency.

- **Alexithymia Spectrum.** Clinical research on alexithymia (difficulty identifying and describing emotions) revealed that emotional awareness exists on a spectrum. Some people want their emotions named and validated; others find emotional commentary intrusive. The axis respects both preferences.

- **Attachment Theory (John Bowlby, 1969; Mary Ainsworth, 1978).** Attachment styles (secure, anxious, avoidant) predict how comfortable individuals are with emotional intimacy and vulnerability. The axis calibrates accordingly.

> Salovey, P., & Mayer, J. D. (1990). Emotional intelligence. *Imagination, Cognition and Personality*, 9(3), 185–211.  
> Goleman, D. (1995). *Emotional Intelligence*. Bantam Books.  
> Bowlby, J. (1969). *Attachment and Loss: Volume 1. Attachment*. Basic Books.

---

### 8. Pace (0 = Measured → 100 = Punchy)

**What it controls:** The rhythm of AI responses — leisurely paragraphs vs. rapid-fire delivery.

**Research foundations:**

- **Communication Accommodation Theory (Howard Giles, 1973).** CAT demonstrates that people adjust their communication style (including pace, vocabulary, and sentence length) to converge with or diverge from their conversation partner. The AI uses this axis to converge with the user's natural tempo.

- **Cognitive Tempo Research.** Research on individual differences in cognitive tempo (fast/impulsive vs. slow/reflective) shows that information processing speed varies significantly between individuals and affects communication preferences.

- **Readability Research (Flesch, 1948; Kincaid et al., 1975).** Decades of readability research show that sentence length and structure directly affect comprehension and engagement. Short sentences create urgency and clarity; longer sentences allow for nuance and flow.

> Giles, H. (1973). Accent mobility: A model and some data. *Anthropological Linguistics*, 15(2), 87–105.  
> Flesch, R. (1948). A new readability yardstick. *Journal of Applied Psychology*, 32(3), 221–233.

---

### 9. Autonomy (0 = Asks Permission → 100 = Takes Initiative)

**What it controls:** Whether the AI waits for explicit instructions or proactively anticipates needs.

**Research foundations:**

- **Self-Determination Theory (Edward Deci & Richard Ryan, 1985).** SDT identifies autonomy as one of three basic psychological needs (alongside competence and relatedness). However, the need manifests differently: some people experience autonomy through making their own choices (preferring the AI to wait), while others experience it through having a proactive partner that anticipates needs.

- **Locus of Control (Julian Rotter, 1966).** Rotter's research showed that individuals vary in whether they believe outcomes are controlled by internal actions or external forces. People with a strong internal locus of control prefer to direct their AI; those with external orientation may appreciate more AI initiative.

- **Interpersonal Circumplex — Agency Axis (Wiggins, 1979).** The vertical axis of the circumplex measures agency/dominance — the degree to which an individual takes charge of interactions. This axis calibrates the AI's position on that same dimension relative to the user.

> Deci, E. L., & Ryan, R. M. (1985). *Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination in Human Behavior*. Springer.  
> Rotter, J. B. (1966). Generalized expectancies for internal versus external control of reinforcement. *Psychological Monographs*, 80(1), 1–28.

---

### 10. Expressiveness (0 = Reserved → 100 = Animated)

**What it controls:** How emotionally vivid and animated the AI's language is.

**Research foundations:**

- **Big Five — Extraversion.** Extraversion encompasses activity level, assertiveness, excitement-seeking, positive emotions, gregariousness, and warmth. Expressiveness maps most closely to the positive emotions and excitement-seeking facets.

- **Emotional Expressivity Research (Kring, Smith, & Neale, 1994).** Research on emotional expressivity showed that it is a stable individual difference — some people are naturally more expressive than others, and they prefer interaction partners who match their level.

- **Social Penetration Theory (Altman & Taylor, 1973).** Altman and Taylor described relationships as moving from surface-level to intimate through gradual self-disclosure. Expressiveness tracks the "breadth" dimension — how much emotional range is expressed at any given depth level.

> Kring, A. M., Smith, D. A., & Neale, J. M. (1994). Individual differences in dispositional expressiveness. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 66(5), 934–949.  
> Altman, I., & Taylor, D. A. (1973). *Social Penetration: The Development of Interpersonal Relationships*. Holt, Rinehart and Winston.

## Design Philosophy

### Why 10 Axes (Not 5, Not 16)?

The Big Five (OCEAN) model provides 5 broad dimensions that are excellent for classification but too coarse for controlling AI behavior. You can't generate a specific communication prompt from just "Extraversion = 72."

Conversely, models like 16PF or MBTI with many dimensions introduce redundancy and make the system harder to calibrate.

**10 axes hit the sweet spot:**
- Enough granularity to produce meaningfully different AI behaviors
- Each axis maps to an **actionable** communication parameter (you can write a distinct prompt rule for each value range)
- No two axes are redundant — each controls a different behavioral dimension
- The system stays interpretable — users can understand what each dial does

### Why Continuous Spectrums (Not Categories)?

Unlike MBTI (which bins people into 16 types) or DISC (4 types), SoulPrint uses continuous 0-100 scales. This reflects the scientific consensus:

> "Personality traits are dimensions, not types. People are not either introverted or extraverted; they fall somewhere on a continuum." — Costa & McCrae, 1992

Continuous values also enable **blending** — the assessment engine and behavioral analyzer can produce fractional scores that are averaged, weighted, and smoothed rather than forced into buckets.

### Dual-Source Architecture

The system derives persona from two complementary sources:

1. **Assessment** (explicit): Structured questions that directly measure psychological dimensions
2. **Behavioral Analysis** (implicit): Statistical analysis of how someone actually communicates

When both are available, they are blended — with assessment data weighted more heavily (0.7 default) because explicit self-report has higher signal-to-noise ratio for personality traits. Behavioral data fills gaps and provides "ground truth" validation of stated preferences.

This dual-source approach mirrors best practices in psychometrics, where multi-method assessment produces more reliable profiles than any single source.
